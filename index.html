The Black Swan

Nassim Taleb's "Black Swan" theory describes rare, unpredictable events with enormous consequences. Migration waves are the result of sudden wars, crises, and disasters — just like black swans.

This series follows a lone figure in a swan costume through two landscapes: an open horizon (instinct, nature, the beginning of the journey) and the cold modernist architecture of the University of Limerick (a symbol of progress where a migrant arrives but never fully belongs).

Artist Statement

Before black swans were found in Australia, their existence seemed impossible — "all swans are white" was simply fact, until it wasn't. Nassim Taleb borrowed that image to describe events that arrive without warning and reshape everything after them: wars, collapses, departures. Migration, in my life and in my family's, has always felt like one of these events.

This series follows a single figure — dressed as a black swan — through two landscapes. On black-and-white film, she moves alone across an open horizon: a body testing the same instinct that sends birds thousands of kilometres toward better ground. In the second act, shot digitally, she carries that instinct into the University of Limerick's glass-and-concrete architecture — a symbol of arrival, of progress, of a world built without her in mind. She belongs fully to neither place.

I shot on black-and-white film to strip away anything that could soften the image, leaving only form, contrast, and the loneliness of a creature who migrated not because she chose ease, but because staying was never really an option.

About

I'm a multidisciplinary artist based in Ireland, currently in my 4th year at LSAD (Limerick School of Art and Design).

My practice examines the intersections of migration, war, propaganda, and identity. I work across photography, video art, installations, and projection mapping — often exploring how political and social narratives shape our sense of belonging.

Recent projects have investigated the visual language of conflict, the role of media in shaping public perception, and the personal experience of displacement. I'm interested in the spaces between truth and fiction, memory and history, home and exile.
